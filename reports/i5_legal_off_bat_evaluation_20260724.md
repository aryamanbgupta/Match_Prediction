# I5 legal-off-bat model: implementation and initial evaluation

Date: 2026-07-24

## Decision

I5 is implemented as an isolated, versioned experimental stack. It is a
correctness improvement over the legacy inclusive-total-runs label contract,
but it is **not promoted over ball-v7 yet**.

The current evidence is mixed:

- I5 improves the raw six-class ball log loss slightly.
- After fitting each model's own validation-only vector calibrator, I5 is
  slightly worse than v3 on ball log loss.
- Raw I5 improves historical match log loss, particularly in the liquid
  slices, but does not improve Brier score on the full slice and does not
  provide robust ROI.
- A paired 30-match prop gate finds no confidence-clean regression in the
  existing guard families, but also no broad predictive improvement.

The consumed 2026-06-02 through 2026-07-13 forward set was not used for I5
training, calibration, tuning, candidate selection, or scoring. I5 needs a
new post-freeze forward window before any promotion decision.

## What changed

I5 uses the versioned delivery contract `legal_off_bat_v1`:

- only legal deliveries enter the six-class ball target;
- the target is off-bat runs, not total team runs;
- raw batter threes are retained in the source/cache, while the current
  six-class target keeps the existing combined 2/3-run class;
- wides and no-balls are generated by a separate validation-fitted empirical
  extras process;
- byes and leg-byes are not credited to the batter or bowler;
- no-ball batter runs accrue to the batter without a ball faced;
- non-striker run-outs are assigned to the dismissed batter and not charged
  to the bowler;
- score, player-card, legal-ball, and outcome-count conservation checks run
  during the cache build.

Production v3/v7 artifacts are untouched. I5 writes to:

- `models/player_stats_cache_i5.sqlite`
- `data/xgb_data_i5/`
- `models/xgb_i5/`
- `eval_out/i5_*`

The empirical extras model is fitted on validation deliveries only. Its event
probabilities are:

| event | probability |
|---|---:|
| legal | 0.957889 |
| wide | 0.037702 |
| no-ball | 0.004409 |

Among legal, non-wicket, off-bat dots, the fitted resolution is 94.434% true
dots, 1.025% byes, and 4.542% leg-byes.

## Data build

The I5 cache contains 9,519 men's matches and 2,106,090 legal outcomes. The
materialized model splits are:

| split | legal balls | raw threes | three rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| train | 1,808,201 | 7,716 | 0.4267% |
| validation | 119,058 | 407 | 0.3419% |
| historical test | 178,831 | 640 | 0.3579% |

Across the full local delivery corpus, threes are 10,463 of 2,477,116 legal
balls (0.4224%). Their rate varies materially by venue:

| venue | threes | legal balls | three rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Melbourne Cricket Ground | 239 | 22,574 | 1.0587% |
| Sydney Cricket Ground | 190 | 19,987 | 0.9506% |
| Shere Bangla National Stadium, Mirpur | 170 | 68,991 | 0.2464% |
| full corpus | 10,463 | 2,477,116 | 0.4224% |

The MCG rate is about 4.3 times the Mirpur rate, consistent with the
hypothesis that usable outfield area and boundary geometry materially affect
whether batters complete a third run. This is descriptive evidence, not a
causal venue estimate: eras, teams, pitch conditions, and competition mix also
differ.

I5 therefore preserves the raw value but does not add a standalone three-run
simulator draw. A seventh modeled outcome would add another sparse probability
to fit and calibrate, plus explicit odd-run strike rotation and more simulator
branches. After the date-normalization hot-path fix, the calibrated I5 prop
path still takes about 9.2 seconds per match at 100 simulations. Adding work to
every simulated delivery for an event occurring on only 0.42% of legal balls
is not a current priority.

Threes remain represented by the combined 2/3-run class, which preserves the
team-run approximation but not the distinction between even- and odd-run
strike rotation. Revisit the sub-draw only after I14 supplies reliable
time-indexed boundary dimensions and an ablation shows that the missing strike
rotation has measurable prop or match-probability value. Physical ground
dimensions are tracked separately as I14 in `research/IDEAS.md`.

## Ball-level results

Lower log loss is better.

| model | validation raw | test raw | validation calibrated | test calibrated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v3/v7 ball model | 1.638883 | 1.629655 | **1.516635** | **1.508998** |
| I5 | **1.633603** | **1.626158** | 1.520215 | 1.514956 |

I5 improves raw test log loss by 0.003497, but is 0.005958 worse after each
model receives its own validation-fitted vector scaler. This does not support
a claim that I5 is currently the better calibrated ball predictor.

The I5 calibrator is fitted on 119,058 validation balls only and saved as
`models/xgb_i5/vector_scaling_calibrator_i5.pkl`.

## Historical match evaluation

This is the already-used 261-match iteration set, not a pristine holdout. Both
rows below are uncalibrated at ball level, making raw I5 versus raw v7 the
clean comparison.

I5's probability metrics are promising in the liquid slices, but no ROI
interval excludes zero.

### Upset sensitivity

The all-liquidity I5 result earns +0.94 units across 255 bets. One 19-unit
France win at 5% market probability supplies more than the total profit.

| removal | I5 ROI | v7 ROI |
|---|---:|---:|
| none | +0.37% | +7.96% |
| largest winning upset | -7.11% | +0.52% |
| three largest winning upsets | -12.74% | -2.08% |
| all winning bets below 25% market probability | -14.09% | -2.08% |

Neither historical ROI is robust to long-shot removal. ROI is therefore a
secondary diagnostic here, not promotion evidence.

Some player families have different row counts between I5 and D15 because the
simulation paths expose different batting/bowling appearances. The paired
analysis excludes a match-family when row counts differ. The full paired
batter-runs comparison contains 2,284 rows; standalone report values must not
be compared directly when their row universes differ.

The earlier n=30 gate appeared neutral because its intervals were wide. The
full run demonstrates why that subset was insufficient: the total-line
regressions become clear at n=261.

### Runtime

Profiling found repeated `datetime.strftime()` calls consumed about 65% of
simulation time. Replacing the equivalent date normalization with
`date().isoformat()` reduced a fixed 100-simulation benchmark from 31.1 to 9.2
seconds (70%) with byte-identical detail JSON. The complete 261-match run took
3,373.7 seconds (56.2 minutes), versus the pre-fix estimate of 2.25-2.7 hours.
An attempted prediction cache preserved seeded output but did not improve
runtime and was removed.

## Current status and next steps

I5 is **implemented, isolated, and tabled as a promotion candidate**:

1. Keep v7 as the current ball/prop model of record.
2. Treat I5 as the cricket-correct foundation for future experiments.
3. Keep the date-normalization hot-path optimization covered by parity tests;
   batch/vectorize XGBoost only if further speed work becomes necessary.
4. Diagnose the scoring-tail regression by ablating the I5 components
   separately: legal/off-bat target, empirical extras, corrected attribution,
   and vector calibration.
5. Do not add a separate three-run draw; it is unrelated to the observed
   total-line regression and would add complexity without evidence.
6. Freeze a new post-2026-07-13 forward window. Use it once, at the end, for
   the selected candidate; never add it to training, validation, calibration,
   or candidate selection.

The incomplete calibrated 261-match match run was interrupted and wrote no
result artifact. Its partial console output is not used in this report. The
full prop result is saved at `models/auto/i5/detail_i5_s43_n261.json`, with
the standalone summary at `reports/i5_prop_report.md`.
