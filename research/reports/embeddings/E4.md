## The finding (this is the E-ladder's headline)

**Given well-shrunk EB marginal distributions, the per-ball T20 outcome
surface is essentially linear, and learned player embeddings add nothing
on average.** Nonlinearity, identity offsets, and extra capacity all fail
to beat multinomial logistic regression at equal information. Per-ball
outcome noise dominates; the extractable identity signal is already
captured by sample-size-adaptive shrinkage of marginal rates.

Consistent with the literature (soccer foundation model: +0.5pp over a
Markov chain) but measured far more cleanly here: same features, same
splits, same target, five frequency buckets, all confounds run down
(state features E2→control; venue parity; capacity).

## What this redirects (not sinks)

The information frontier for cricket models is NOT better point-estimate
player representation — it is (a) within-innings sequence context, which
no EB feature captures and which is exactly the T-rungs' hypothesis;
(b) downstream uses where similarity/geometry matter (roles, scouting,
valuation) — still unproven, probes say current vectors carry no
attribute structure; (c) calibration/simulation coherence for rollouts.
The paper story sharpens: "we quantify how far shrunk marginals go
(very far), and show where the remaining information lives."
