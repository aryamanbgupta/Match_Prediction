# A6 — New data: historical weather (dew proxy)

**Status: FAILED** (neither gate metric improved)
**Run:** 2026-07-11, overnight AutoResearch iteration.

## Hypothesis
Evening humidity / dew at the venue affects chase advantage (wet ball harder
to grip and field with in the second innings), which the model can't currently
see. Strongest for night games. A dew signal, oriented toward the chasing
team, should add winner-market accuracy the additive ELO/venue features miss.

## What was done
- **GET-only external data** from open-meteo (no new deps, no uploads):
  - **Geocoding** (`geocoding-api.open-meteo.com`) of all 464 distinct venues
    in train/val/test via a city-after-comma → cleaned-name → full-name
    candidate cascade. Coverage **340/464 = 73%** (stadium/school-ground names
    that carry no city are the misses). Cached to
    `data/external/weather/geocode.json`.
  - **Historical archive** (`archive-api.open-meteo.com`) hourly
    `relative_humidity_2m, temperature_2m, dew_point_2m`, `timezone=auto`, one
    call per venue over its match-date span. Cached under
    `data/external/weather/archive/` (~450 MB, resumable). A handful of venues
    hit HTTP 429 on the first pass; a second (cache-warm) pass cleared them.
  - Evening window = local hours **18:00–22:00**, mean per (venue, date).
- **Joined onto the existing materialized parquet** (`data/xgb_match_data_v2_clean`)
  by (venue, match_date) → `data/auto/a6/{train,validation,test}.parquet`. No
  career re-materialization needed — weather is a per-(venue,date) scalar.
  Weather present: train 76.6%, val 95.2%, **test 89.9%** (missing → NaN, which
  XGB routes natively). Builder: `scripts/auto/a6_weather.py` (reverted).
- **3 features added:**
  - `wx_evening_humidity` — mean evening RH% (symmetric, condition level)
  - `wx_evening_dewpoint` — mean evening dew point °C (symmetric)
  - `wx_dew_adv_team1` — **oriented** dew advantage =
    `(RH − train_mean_RH) × (1 − 2·team1_batting_first)`. Dew helps the chaser;
    team1 chases when it does not bat first. `team1_batting_first` is a toss
    feature already in the model → known pre-match, temporally clean. Centering
    constant is train-only (`train_mean_RH = 68.11%`).

## Correlation check (repo discipline, before the verdict)
Computed on the 6,063 train rows with weather present:

| feature | \|r\| vs target (floor 0.03) | max \|r\| vs existing (flag 0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| wx_evening_humidity | 0.0041 | 0.130 (team1_bowling_elo) |
| wx_evening_dewpoint | 0.0075 | 0.118 (team1_bottom5_bowling_elo_avg) |
| wx_dew_adv_team1 | **0.0049** | 0.137 (team1_bowling_elo) |

All three **PASS redundancy** (genuinely new information, \|r\|≤0.14 vs every
existing feature) but **FAIL the M6 target-corr floor** — even the oriented
dew-advantage feature carries essentially no match-level signal (r = +0.0049
vs the 0.03 floor). This is the identical M3–M6 / A5 pattern.

## What I'd try next
The dew hypothesis dies at the same wall as every other additive match-level
direction (M3–M6, A5): the signal is real physics but is (a) too weak at
match-level aggregation and (b) already implicitly priced by
`venue_chase_win_pct` + `team1_batting_first`, which a depth-4 tree can bend by
venue. Two non-obvious follow-ups worth filing rather than re-running weather
as-is: (1) restrict the dew feature to the subset where it should bite — night
matches at high-dew venues — but we lack reliable start-time data, so this needs
a start-time source first; (2) weather is far more likely to matter at
**ball-level** (second-innings scoring collapse under dew) than at match-level —
a dew covariate in the v7 sim's second-innings scoring is a cleaner test of the
same physics, and lives in the sim/prop gate rather than the winner-market gate.
Neither is a quick match-level retrain, so both are new PENDING candidates, not
this idea re-run.
