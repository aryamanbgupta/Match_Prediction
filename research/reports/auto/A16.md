# A16 — Sparse regime-change-over ball calibrator {0, 6, 15} (A15 extension)

**Verdict: FAILED** (2026-07-11). Resumed an in-flight iteration that had
claimed A16, fit the {0,6,15} calibrator, logged the pre-run divergences, and
launched the n=261 sim; this iteration waited for that sim to finish, ran the

## Hypothesis

A15 LANDED a minimal **over-0-only** vector calibrator that captured the full
`team_first_over_mae` win (dMAE −0.018, CI excludes 0) with 2 effective vectors,
while A14's blanket 19 other per-over vectors were inert/overfit. A16 asks
whether the *other* "regime-change" overs carry a similar mechanically-distinct
miscalibration that a **sparse** calibrator could fix:

- **over 0** — first ball a fresh batter + new bowler face (A15's win)
- **over 6** — start of the middle overs (field restriction lifts, bowling change)
- **over 15** — start of the death (specialist death bowlers enter)

The ~17 non-boundary overs stay on the global vector (A15 discipline: add a
vector only where it is mechanically distinct and observable). The decisive
question is **not** "does A16 beat the single global vector" (over 0 alone
already does, per A15) but "do overs 6 and 15 add anything **observable beyond
over 0**?"

## Verdict rationale

- **GATE 1 (real incremental lift on a death/middle prop beyond A15) — NOT met.**
  Of 10 death/middle/pp families in the incremental comparison, only 2 cross
  significance — `highest_over_runs_ou_24_5` (−0.0008) and `pp_total_ou_55_5`
  (−0.0009) — and **both have CI upper bounds sitting exactly at the noise
  boundary (−0.0000)**. This is fully consistent with multiple-comparisons
  chance (testing 10 families at 95% CI). Decisively, **`pp_total_ou_55_5` is a
  powerplay prop (overs 0–5); overs 6 & 15 are outside the powerplay and cannot
  mechanically move it** — its marginal "significance" is downstream Monte-Carlo
  noise (second-innings PP batting responds to a different first-innings total),
  which confirms the crossings are noise, not signal. No family shows a clean
  above-noise incremental gain comparable to A15's over-0 win
  (`team_first_over_mae` −0.018 with a comfortable margin, ~20–25× the magnitude
  and CI well clear of 0).
- **GATE 2 (guards) — HELD**, but this is a no-regression safety condition, not
  an improvement.
- Zero improvements → **FAILED** (honoring the idea's pre-committed rule: "if
  {0,6,15} only reproduces A15's over-0 gain with no incremental lift → FAILED").
  A16 is strictly dominated by the simpler A15: A15 + 2 inert vectors, i.e. pure
  extra overfitting surface. Nothing to TABLE — it moved no useful metric worth
  recombining.

**Why overs 6 & 15 wash out (reproduces A8/A12).** Their marginal-vector
divergences (0.138, 0.078) *exceed* the ~0.05 A8/A12 washout threshold, yet on
multi-over **aggregated** props the small per-ball corrections at just 2 of 20
overs still net to noise. A15's over-0 fix is the exception precisely because it
feeds a **single-over** prop (`team_first_over_mae`) where the over-0 balls are
the *entire* prop — no aggregation to wash it out. Overs 6 and 15 have **no
equivalent single-over prop** that isolates them (`highest_over_runs` is the
max over, not a fixed over; `pp_total` is overs 0–5), so their incremental lift
cannot be isolated the way over-0's could. The washout threshold is about the
*marginal vector*, but the *observable* threshold is set by whether a prop
isolates the fixed over — and only over 0 has one.

**A15 stands as the recommended minimal first-over calibrator.** Over 0 is the
sole regime-change over whose miscalibration is both large *and* isolable by a
prop.

## What I'd try next

The per-over linear-scaling lever is now well-characterized: it only produces an
**observable** prop gain when a fixed-over prop isolates the corrected over
(over 0 / `team_first_over_mae`). Two directions that avoid the washout trap:

1. **A13 (dispersion calibration)** — still PENDING and orthogonal to all of
   A8/A14/A15/A16. Those all correct the *marginal rate* per ball; A13 corrects
   the *variance* of sampled totals (the sim under-disperses: `team_first_over`
   P10–P90 coverage 48%, `batter_runs` 74% vs ideal 80%). Under-dispersion is
   exactly what inflates over/under tail Brier and is untouched by any rate
   scaler. This is the highest-value remaining sim lever.
2. If per-over scaling is revisited, target it at a **fixed-over prop that
   isolates over 6 or over 15** (e.g. an "over-6 runs" or "over-15 runs" family)
   rather than an aggregate — but note no such prop currently exists in
   `prop_backtest.py`, and adding prop families is out of scope for the frozen
   eval. Absent an isolating prop, the per-over lever is exhausted at over 0.
