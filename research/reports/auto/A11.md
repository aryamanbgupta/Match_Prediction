A7 landed the slice-conditional edge threshold with the mismatch/close boundary
fixed at `|top6_batting_elo_diff| = 5` (inherited from M8) and edge > 10%. A7

## Interpretation

The two slices point in **opposite directions**, which is exactly what the
dual-slice bar was designed to catch:

- **≥$50k ROI is monotone-decreasing in the boundary** (b3 +40.96 → b12 +27.73):
  a tighter cut labels more fixtures "mismatch" and filters more aggressively
  (b3 drops 78 of 168), which raises flat ROI on the noisier ≥$50k pool.
- **≥$100k ROI peaks at b=5** (+35.86). On the sharper, more liquid market the
  aggressive b3 filter *over-culls* — ROI falls to +32.56% and its CI reopens
  across 0 ([−6.59, +83.70]). The ≥$100k market — where the model's edge is
  most real — endorses boundary 5 as its optimum.
- **Win rate is flat (~54–56%) across every boundary and both slices** — the ROI
  differences come entirely from *which* bets are dropped, not a win-rate shift.
  So b3's ≥$50k gain is a pnl-mix effect on a thin residual, not sharper
  discrimination.

The honest read: boundary 5 is robust. It is the ≥$100k optimum and second-best
(within one drop) on ≥$50k, and no cut improves both. A11 converts A7's
inherited constant into a *validated* choice rather than replacing it.

## What I'd try next

The only cut that beats 5 anywhere is b3 on ≥$50k (+4pp) — but it does so by
throwing away ~half the book (n 168→90) and its ≥$100k edge evaporates, so it is
a variance play, not signal. A joint (boundary × edge) 2-D sweep is the obvious
next lever but is high slice-shopping risk and A7 already showed edge is robust;
not worth queueing on this evidence. A cleaner future direction is a
**liquidity-conditional** rule (different filter aggressiveness for ≥$50k vs
≥$100k), but that must be pre-registered and forward-tested — the ≥$100k
sample (n≈72 after filtering) is too thin to fit a second free parameter
without over-fitting. Boundary 5 / edge 10% remains the recommended production
sizing rule.
