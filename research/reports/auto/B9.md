# B9 — top_bowler margin vs a usage-share fair baseline (B4 follow-up)

**Verdict: LANDED** (instrumentation+verdict hybrid gate: analysis completed
with real numbers; conclusion stated with its CI). **The margin FLIPS.**

## Hypothesis

B4 showed the E2 career-wickets-share baseline is weak exactly where the
sim's top_bowler profit concentrates (p_base<2% longshots): it prices
debutants at 0.26% when they actually top the wickets 0.90% of the time,
because career-wickets share cannot see *who actually bowls*. If the sim's
CI-clean ΔBrier margin (B4: −0.0017 on the D15 engine) survives a stronger
"competent bettor" baseline — as-of expected-overs share × career wicket
rate — the E2/E5 skill claim is robust; if it flips, "first binary family
with real skill" needs a caveat and the E2 bar needs interactive revision.

(date, deliveries bowled, BOWLER_KINDS wickets) — crucially including
**zero-usage appearances** for XI members who never bowl. Baseline price for

- expected deliveries = EB-shrunk mean deliveries per appearance
  (K_USAGE = 5 appearances) toward the as-of global mean per XI appearance
  (lineup-uniform prior — a true debutant sits at the prior);
- wicket rate = EB-shrunk per-delivery bowler-credited rate
  (K_RATE = 120 deliveries) toward the as-of global rate;
- p_usage ∝ product, normalized within the market's rows.

match, 2,000 resamples, seed 29). B4's career-baseline pairing reproduces
**exactly** (|Δ| = 0.00e+00 vs `models/auto/b4/pricing_numbers.json`).

## Reading

1. **E5's "first binary family to beat a fair baseline" is falsified at the
   competent-bettor bar.** The sim's top_bowler edge existed only relative
   to a baseline that couldn't see who bowls. A simple as-of
   expected-deliveries × wicket-rate model beats the calibrated sim
   CI-clean, in every band that matters, at every shrinkage setting tried.
2. **B4's pricing table was a baseline artifact.** The +138…+166% ROI grid
   inverts to −29…−45% when the synthetic bookmaker prices with usage. The
   "honest bettable slice" framing of B4 (5–10% band) does not survive
   either — no vig×threshold cell is +EV against the stronger book.
3. The two sim distortions B4 saw entangled are now split cleanly, and both
   are **usage** errors, not rate errors: the sim under-bowls true debutants
   (1.63% priced vs 8.47% actual — the lineup-uniform prior alone nails them
   at 9.06%), and it over-bowls experienced non-bowlers (1.29% vs 0.62%
   actual; D5's keepers). Who-bowls, not per-ball rates, is the binding
   defect for bowler-family props.
4. E2's bar (`prop_fair_baselines.py`) is loop-forbidden → flagged for
   interactive revision (new backlog entry I13): top_bowler's fair baseline
   should become the usage-share model; bowler_wkts families likely need the
   same upgrade (expected-balls × rate via a count model).

## What I'd try next

Sim-side: align the sim's who-bowls distribution to as-of usage
expectations — raise unknown/debutant bowling share toward the
lineup-uniform prior and cut long-history zero-usage players toward ~0
(mechanism-level superset of D5's threshold filter; appended as B10). The
gate target is closing the +0.0038 gap to the usage baseline on this exact
statistic. Separately, B3 (continuous shrinkage blend) generalizes: a val-fit
blend of sim and usage baseline would give the best deployable top_bowler
quote even before the sim itself improves — but with the sim CI-clean worse
pooled AND in the head, the useful blend weight may be near 0; measure, not
assume.
