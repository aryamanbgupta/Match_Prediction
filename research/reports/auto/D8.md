Train spans 2005–2024 uniformly weighted (`model.fit` passed no
`sample_weight`); T20 is non-stationary (base-rate drift, scoring-era shift).
Exponential time-decay on the training loss concentrates capacity on the

loss and adds no features, so the correlation discipline doesn't apply.

## Reading

Recency weighting is a real accuracy lever — the val sweep has an interior
optimum at HL≈6y and the LL gain transfers to the held-out iteration slice
on every seed at both volume slices. But sharper probabilities again fail to
move flat ROI (A4/D8 pattern: ROI is a threshold function of side-vs-market,
and the sides barely change — win rate is flat). The one metric it clearly
buys, LL stability and level, is exactly what D7's swap augmentation also
buys, which makes the two natural stacking partners: they act through
disjoint mechanisms (loss weighting vs data augmentation) on the same
training procedure.

## What I'd try next

The obvious combination: **D7 swap augmentation + D8 HL=6 decay** (appended
as D13). Swap-augment supplies the ROI arm D8 lacks (+3.0/+3.4pp in
D7/D12) and both improve LL independently; the decay weights apply
unchanged to mirrored rows (same match_date). If the LL gains even
partially stack, the combined model would sit well below market LL. Also
worth noting for any production adoption discussion: HL was selected on the
2024-12→2025-06 val window; a live deployment should re-check the HL choice
as the window rolls forward, since the optimum plausibly tracks how fast
the game drifts.
