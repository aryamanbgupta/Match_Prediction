# B4 — top_bowler pricing margin (post-calibration edge quantification)

**Verdict: LANDED** (instrumentation gate, A1/D10-style: pricing table +
report produced with real numbers). 2026-07-21.

## Hypothesis

top_bowler is the only binary prop family beating its fair baseline (E5:
−0.0023 [−0.0038, −0.0008] paired ΔBrier at n=261, calibrated sim).
Quantify that margin as *implied odds*: at what synthetic-market price does
the sim's top_bowler probability have positive EV vs the fair baseline as
market maker? Output a pricing table, not a model change.

## What I did

Pure analysis — no sim run, no model/engine change. Harness
`scripts/auto/b4_pricing_margin.py` (committed before the run; extended
mid-analysis with the band/D5 diagnostics at `b387cbb`):

- **Inputs**: canonical venue-ON D15 detail JSON
  `models/auto/d15/detail_d15_s43_n261.json` (n=261, seed 43, current
  default engine: venue encoder + run_rate fix + D15 attribution unit,
  v1 vector calibrator = calibrated sim). Context arm: same-seed D1 detail
  (pre-attribution-unit engine). Fair baseline: the exact E2 machinery
  (`prop_fair_baselines.py` imported read-only; corpus cache reused;
  career-wickets share within XI, strictly as-of).
- **Market model** (fixed pre-run): synthetic bookmaker quotes
  q_i = p_base_i × (1+vig) (multiplicative overround), decimal odds 1/q_i;
  sim bets YES-only, flat 1u, when p_sim/q − 1 > threshold; settles on
  cricsheet y. Cluster-bootstrap ROI CIs by match (2,000 resamples, seed 29).
- 522 team-markets, 5,835 player rows; sim and baseline probabilities both
  sum to 1.000 per market (hard-checked). 6 markets have no y=1 (zero
  bowler-credited wickets).

| <2% (odds > ~50) | 1240 | +281.26 | [+123.89, +459.26] | +3487.7 |

The edge is a **longshot phenomenon**: 83% of total PnL comes from
p_base < 2%, and the sim adds *nothing at the head* (10–20% band flat;
≥20% band −21% point estimate). The tail profit has two entangled sources,
both audited row-by-row:

1. **The career-wickets-share baseline catastrophically underprices
   debutants.** Zero-career-wicket players (1,661 rows) top the wickets at
   a 0.90% base rate but are priced at mean 0.26%. All 15 such winners are
   genuine debutants (verified in-corpus, wickets only at/after the eval
   date — no name-mismatch artifact); at odds of hundreds they swamp
   everything.
2. **The sim wildly overprices non-bowlers** — the D5 pathology, now in
   betting units: 477 rows give a zero-career-wicket player p_sim ≥ 2%
   (mean 3.84%), topped by pure batters/keepers (PD Salt p_sim 6%,
   S Konstas 9%, Sahibzada Farhan 10%, DP Conway 5–6%, SD Hope 8%) whom
   the baseline correctly prices at ~0.1%. Vs *this* baseline those bets
   still profit (+309% — the debutant underpricing in the same bucket is
   even larger), but vs a real book they are pure giveaway.

## Reading

- The honest answer to "at what price does the sim have positive EV":
  vs the E2 baseline as market maker, at any vig up to 50% — but that says
  more about the baseline's tail weakness than about bettable edge. The
  defensible bettable slice is the **5–10% implied-prob band (odds ~10–21):
  ROI +54% CI [+23, +85]** — quotable prices, CI-clean, and not driven by
  the debutant artifact (players there have real careers).
- The E2 fair-baseline bar, which every binary family is judged against,
  is **weak in the tail**: any model that knows *who actually bowls*
  (lineup/usage) beats career-wickets share on debutants. A usage-share
  baseline is the right stronger bar (appended as B9).
- **D5 is now quantified**: the eligibility filter has a concrete target
  (477 p_sim ≥ 2% zero-career rows; keepers with 6–12% top-bowler prob)
  and a measurable success criterion beyond prop Briers.
