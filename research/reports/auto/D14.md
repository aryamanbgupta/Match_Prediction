## Hypothesis

D2 TABLED with a lone CI-clean `team_first_over_mae` regression despite
provably-correct `update()` semantics. The suspected culprit: a
pre-existing attribution bug — `_simulate_innings` keys its per-ball
batting card on `state.striker_idx` AFTER `update()` has rotated strike /
replaced a dismissed batter. The two bugs are entangled; fix the card and
re-apply D2 together, gated as one unit.

## What I did

1. Re-applied D2 (`git revert bc44473` → `7079c96`): strike rotates on odd
   off-the-bat runs only; extras charge the striker neither runs nor a
   ball faced.
2. **Pre-ball attribution snapshot** in `_simulate_innings` (`3cb2f7e`).
   Scope statement (declared pre-run in the gate script): the
   read-after-update defect touches more than the striker key, and it was
   fixed completely rather than half-per-field:
   - **Batting card** keyed on the striker who faced the delivery
     (pre-rotation/replacement).
   - **Bowling card** keyed on the delivering bowler — `simulate_ball`
     reassigns `state.bowler_idx` to the NEXT over's bowler at over end
     BEFORE the card read, so the 6th legal ball of every over (runs + any
     wicket) was credited to the wrong bowler (~1/6 of all deliveries).
   - **BallResult over/ball labels** pre-increment — `state.balls` is
     already incremented at read time, so each over's 6th legal delivery
     was labeled into the NEXT over. `prop_backtest`'s first-over sum
     (`b.over == 0`) was therefore systematically missing its final
     delivery (and powerplay/highest-over grouping shifted likewise).
   - `team_runs`/`team_wickets` stay cumulative POST-ball (the
     first-wicket extractor documents and depends on that).

## Reading

Three separate findings, one mechanism each:

1. **D2's first-over regression is resolved** — and inverted. The
   over-label fix restores the missing 6th delivery to the `b.over == 0`
   extraction window and `team_first_over_mae` improves 3.526 → 3.395
   (−0.132 CI-clean), ~7× the magnitude of A15's over-0 calibrator gain
   (−0.018). Part of what the A14/A15 first-over calibrators (six/wicket
   ×1.23) and B7's refit were compensating was plausibly this truncated
   extraction window, not model miscalibration.
2. **Batter-card mis-keying nets ~zero on batter families** across 100
   sims (batter_runs_mae +0.000): striker swaps are frequent enough that
   per-batter attribution errors wash out in distribution. D2's original
   regression really was the label/extraction channel, not the batter
   card.
3. **The bowler-card fix unmasks the D4 overshoot.** Correct over-final
   keying concentrates each bowler's wickets on their own card (the old
   cross-bowler smearing of every over's last ball diluted per-bowler
   wicket counts). The sim's bowler-wicket rates residually overshoot
   (E5: bowler_wkts_1plus skill −0.087 vs base rate; D4's diagnosis: 100%
   of dismissals credited to the bowler, no run-out channel), so
   sharpening attribution makes the overshoot MORE visible → +0.0027
   CI-clean. The mis-keying was accidentally cancelling part of a known
   bias — the same entanglement pattern as D2→D14, one layer down.
