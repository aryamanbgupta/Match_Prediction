## Hypothesis (from IDEAS I14)

The learned venue embedding cannot generalize physical ground
characteristics to venues it has barely seen. Concatenating registry
physicals (boundary midpoints, altitude, monthly climate normals, known
flags) should improve low-history-venue prediction without hurting
elsewhere.

## Setup

Teacher-forced paired per-ball NLL on the identical 186,667-ball test
split (822 matches, 134 venues); cluster bootstrap by match (2000/s29).
Feature coverage on the frame: boundary 29–38% of balls, altitude/climate
62–70% (registry = 119 of 373 canonical venues, top-volume-first).
Training: val LL 1.4339 (production 1.4334), test LL 1.4254 (1.4253) —
aggregate flat, as expected for a low-history-targeted feature set.

Read: the hypothesis is *narrowly right* — physical features produce a
real improvement exactly in the data-starved band the embedding can't
cover (1–5 matches), and a tiny penalty where the embedding is data-rich
(21–100). The primary failed because the ≤20 pool is diluted by the
0-match bucket, where the features can't help **for a coverage reason**:
unseen venues are mostly obscure grounds that are not in the 119-venue
registry either (boundary coverage on test is 29% of balls), so their
`vphys_*` values are NaN.

## What this closes and what it opens

Closed: naive global concatenation of registry physicals onto the
production ball model does not clear the declared low-history gate and
ships nothing. The registry remains a data asset (and the I5 three-run
geometry input) — no model integration.

Opened (follow-ups recorded in IDEAS I14, each needs its own
pre-committed gate before any retry):
1. **Coverage-first retry:** extend registry coords/climate into the
   sparse tail (the 20–39-match band and below — the 0-bucket is where
   the gate was lost, and it is a rows problem again, like pass 3.5).
2. **History-gated features:** apply/learn `vphys_*` only where venue
   history is thin (e.g. interaction with a train-match-count feature),
   so the 21–100 penalty cannot offset the sparse-band gain.
3. Full grouped-by-venue holdout training (the I14 text's stronger
   design) — this test used the temporal split with venue-history
   slicing, the cheap first look.

Caveats: one seed, one config; teacher-forced only (no sim-path wiring —
deliberately deferred until a ball-level gate passes).
