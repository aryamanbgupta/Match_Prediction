## Hypothesis

B10/B12 (shipped) share-matched the `EmpiricalBowlerSelector` usage-absent
branch to B9's as-of expected balls, fixing debutants (defect a) but moving
veteran never-bowlers (defect b) the WRONG way: a ≥20-app, 0-career-ball
player gets `k_u·prior/(k_u+n)` ≈ 1–2 expected balls, so that cohort's XI
share rose 0.270% → 0.496% (actual ≈ 0). A beta-geometric damping —
`P(bowls | n apps, 0 balls) = k/(k+n)`, fit as-of — should send that cohort
toward its true ≈0 share while leaving debutants and usage-present players
byte-untouched, closing part of the residual sim−usage `top_bowler` margin
and B4's non-bowler tail (PD Salt at p_sim 6%, etc.).

## Reading

The mechanism worked exactly as designed and bought nothing measurable. The
cohort is too small to matter at the market layer: 1,059 of 5,835
player-market rows, each already priced near zero by BOTH the sim and the B9
usage baseline — removing a ~0.5% share from those rows redistributes ~0.09pp
of innings share, and the baseline barely moves, so the margin is unchanged
(the +6.5e-5 "growth" is coin-flip scale against ~±0.0009-wide CIs, but the
pre-committed point test fails honestly). This is the reading B13's own
FAILED clause anticipated: the non-bowler `top_bowler` tail is a
**per-ball-rate** problem (what the model predicts when a non-bowler does
bowl), not a **who-bowls** problem. With B10/B12 (debutants) landed and B13
(never-bowlers) null, the who-bowls lever is now fully explored: selector
share alignment is done.

Costs recorded for any future revisit: 24 extra relaxation fallbacks (damping
pushes never-bowlers below `min_share=0.01`, dropping thin squads under 5
eligible — `min_share` would need re-deriving jointly with damping), and an
unpinned collateral shift of the `other_unknown` cohort (5.548% → 3.487%).
